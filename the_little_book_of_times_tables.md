# The Little Book of Times Tables

This is a little book about how to beat the system and triumph over a world of tyranny and stupidity.

There is a little test on the last page of the book. Please do not read the last page until you have read the rest of the book. If you have understood the ideas in the book the test should be easy.

## The Beginning

One day, when I was about your age, I decided that learning my times tables was not for me and I was not going to do it. 

Mrs Saunders had just announced that we all had to memorize our times tables and made us recite them over and over - '1x2 is 2, 2x2 is 4 ...'

Mrs Saunders was the enemy.

This left me with a problem. After the usual recital Mrs Saunders would pick on individuals in the class and fire multiplication questions at them. The others seemed to have no problem remembering '6x8 is ..' but what would I do when she picked on me?

I decided it could not be too hard to just work it out in my head on-the-fly. Calculators hadn't yet been invented but I knew how to add and subtract numbers. If I added up enough numbers quickly enough I'd get my answers. What could possibly go wrong?

## 2x table

Next the 2x table. 

2 x 2 is the same as 2 + 2 which is 4. We can write that like:

    2 x 2 = 4

2 x 3 is the same as 3 + 3 which is 6.

    2 x 3 = 6

And so on:

    2 x 4 = 8
    2 x 5 = 10
    2 x 6 = 12
    2 x 7 = 14
    2 x 8 = 16
    2 x 9 = 18
    2 x 12 = 24

We already know 2 x 10 and 2 x 11.

So far so good.

## 3x table

What about the 3x table? 

I reckoned 3 was the same a 2 + 1 so if I just added another number to my 2x table I'd get my answer. For example:

    3 x 6 = (2 + 1) x 6
          = 2 x 6 + 1 x 6
          = 12 + 6
          = 18

So 3x needed a bit more work, but that was easy too.

## 5x table

The interesting thing about 5 is that two of them make ten. If you want to multiply 5 by a number you can find out how many twos go into it to get the number of 10s; then if the remainder is 0 the result ends in 0 and if the remainder is 1 the result ends in 5. 
So to do 5 x 7 we can get three twos in 7 and the remainder is 1 so the result is 35.

## 7x and 8x tables

That leaves us with 7s and 8s and 12 x 12 (which was just too hard).
I could get the 8x by doubling the 4x but that would mean a lot of mental arithmetic. At this point I gave in to Mrs Saunders and memorized the last few calculations:

    7 x 7 = 49
    7 x 8 = 56
    8 x 8 = 64
    12 x 12 = 144
